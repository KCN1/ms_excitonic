However, the limitations of the nearest-neighbor tight-binding model applied to these finite oligomers (\(N=1\dots5\)) should be explicitly outlined. First, the tight-binding framework represents a limiting case for systems with localized excitations, whereas the non-zero through-space \(\pi \)-\(\pi \) orbital overlap in ZnPc stacks introduces a minor degree of electron delocalization. Second, while short-range interactions decay exponentially and are well-restricted to the nearest neighbors, the long-range Coulombic coupling decays as \(1/r\) and ideally requires accounting for next-nearest-neighbor interactions. 

Secondly, t assumes that the coupling is strictly localized between nearest neighbors. In a real crystal lattice, the finite-size truncation and the omission of long-range electrostatic contributions across multiple unit cells may lead to an underestimation of the total band dispersion.

Finally, an inherent limitation of the 1D tight-binding extrapolation lies in the blurring boundary between localized molecular aggregates and extended \(\pi \)-conjugated systems.\({}^{39}\) Due to the noticeable spatial \(\pi \)-\(\pi \) orbital overlap in close-packed ZnPc stacks, a degree of through-space electron delocalization occurs. This delocalization transitions the system behavior toward band-like transport typical of fully conjugated structures,\({}^{39,43}\) which cannot be comprehensively described solely by traditional localized exciton interaction models (such as Coulombic and short-range exchange/superexchange components) and limits the quantitative accuracy of the nearest-neighbor approximation.»
