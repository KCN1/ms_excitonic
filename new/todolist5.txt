However, several inherent limitations of this idealized nearest-neighbour tight-binding approach should be addressed explicitly. First, the tight-binding framework represents a limiting case for systems with localized excitations. The non-zero through-space $\pi$-$\pi$ orbital overlap in ZnPc stacks introduces a minor degree of electron delocalization, transitioning the system behavior toward a band-like regime.${}^{39,43}$ In heavily delocalized systems, the energy gap scaling typically deviates from the linear $\cos[\pi/(N+1)]$ dependence and shifts toward a particle-in-a-box type $1/N$ behavior. Second, while short-range exchange and overlap interactions decay exponentially and are tightly restricted to the nearest neighbors, long-range Coulombic coupling scales as $1/r$ and theoretically necessitates the inclusion of next-nearest-neighbour (NNN) terms. Within the finite series up to $N=5$, the NNN contributions would manifest as a quadratic correction to Equation (2). Nevertheless, the excellent linearity of the experimental and calculated data in $\cos[\pi/(N+1)]$ coordinates ($R^2 \sim 0.998$) underscores the minor contribution of these higher-order effects within the investigated size range, validating the truncation of the model.
Finally, the one-dimensional nearest-neighbour approximation intrinsically neglects inter-stack interactions. In a realistic molecular aggregate, each ZnPc unit is surrounded by multiple adjacent neighbors across neighboring columns. Although the inter-stack distance is sufficiently large to weaken the Coulombic coupling and minimize orbital overlap, a comprehensive quantitative description would require simulating multidimensional clusters (e.g., $2\times2$, $2\times3$, or $2\times2\times2$ configurations) to explicitly map the multi-center interaction network.${}^{41,73}$

However, this 1D nearest-neighbour approximation assumes strict additivity of pairwise interactions, which strictly holds only in the perturbative limit where intermolecular charge-transfer (CT) states remain high-energy virtual states.${}^{43}$ Significant $\pi$-$\pi$ orbital overlap can reduce the exciton binding energy, inducing a transition toward a band-like regime where $\Delta E$ scales non-linearly as $1/N$ instead of $\cos[\pi/(N+1)]$. Furthermore, while short-range interactions decay exponentially, long-range Coulombic coupling scales as $1/r$, theoretically introducing next-nearest-neighbour terms. Nevertheless, the excellent linearity of our oligomer data ($R^2 \sim 0.998$) for $N \le 5$ proves that such higher-order effects are negligible within this size range, validating the truncation. Finally, a fully quantitative description would require transitioning from a 1D chain to multidimensional clusters (e.g., $2\times2$ or $2\times3$) to explicitly map weak but multi-center inter-stack interactions.${}^{41,73}$"
